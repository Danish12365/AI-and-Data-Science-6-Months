
The sum of absolute differences (SAD):
The sum of absolute differences (SAD) is a metric used to measure the similarity between two images, image blocks, or signals by calculating the sum of the absolute differences
between corresponding elements. It is widely used in areas like digital image processing, motion estimation, and stereovision, and is also a computational technique for solving 
various problems, such as finding the sum of absolute differences of indices in an array. 


The "sum of squared distances" is a statistical measure of variability that calculates the sum of the squared differences between each data point and a reference point, 
often the mean. This process involves subtracting the reference value from each data point, squaring each result, and then adding all the squared values together. 
It is used to quantify the dispersion of a data set and is fundamental to methods like regression analysis, where it helps measure how well a model fits the data. 
How to calculate the sum of squares
Gather your data points: Collect all the individual values in your data set.
Calculate the mean: Find the average of your data set.
Subtract the mean: For each data point, subtract the mean from it.
Square the results: Square each of the differences you found in the previous step.
Sum the squares: Add all the squared values together to get the final sum of squares.
